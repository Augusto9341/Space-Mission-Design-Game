
# Houston, We Have a Problem.

## Overview

**Houston, We Have a Problem** is a 2D point-and-click game developed with the Godot Engine and C# for the NASA Space Apps Challenge.

Inspired by the complexities of operating a scientific space mission, the game places players aboard a spacecraft where they must monitor critical systems, respond to unexpected emergencies, and make decisions under time pressure.

The project explores one of the central challenges of space exploration: **keeping a mission operational while managing limited resources and competing priorities.**

Our development was guided by NASA's educational material [**Design and Develop Science Missions**](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/250323-design-and-develop-science-mission.pdf?emrc=a454cf), which explores the engineering decisions, resource constraints, and scientific considerations involved in planning and operating space missions.

While the challenge encompasses the entire mission lifecycle, our project focuses specifically on the operational side: the difficulties of maintaining spacecraft systems, protecting scientific experiments, and responding to unforeseen events.

## Gameplay

Players begin in the spacecraft's cockpit and must navigate between different areas to monitor and maintain its systems.

Throughout the mission, random emergencies can occur and sometimes overlap, forcing players to prioritize tasks and manage multiple threats simultaneously.

To restore damaged systems and keep the spacecraft operational, players must interact with control panels, activate switches, replace damaged fuses, and solve mathematical challenges.

These challenges incorporate mathematical concepts into the spacecraft's maintenance procedures, including:

- Linear equations and functions.
- Quadratic equations using the sum and product of roots.
- Notable algebraic identities.
- Basic trigonometric identities.

The objective is to survive for as long as possible while keeping the spacecraft operational and protecting its scientific experiments.

## Emergency Systems

### Power and Scientific Experiments

Power failures can interrupt the spacecraft's scientific experiments, represented initially by biological samples.

Without a stable power supply, the integrity of these samples gradually decreases. Players must restore power before the samples are irreversibly lost.

Their integrity can be monitored through the onboard tablet.

### Oxygen

Oxygen system failures require immediate attention. Players must reach the appropriate control panel and solve a mathematical challenge to restore the system before the oxygen supply is depleted.

### Navigation

Trajectory deviations can compromise the entire mission. Players must use the spacecraft's navigation controls to correct its course before it remains off trajectory for too long.

### Meteoroid Threats

An audible warning alerts players to incoming meteoroids.

They must quickly reach the appropriate control panel and activate the spacecraft's defense system before impact.

## Game Over Conditions

The mission ends if any of the following conditions occur:

- The integrity of the scientific experiments reaches zero.
- The spacecraft remains off course for too long.
- A meteoroid collides with the spacecraft.
- The oxygen supply is depleted.

## Use of AI

Our team used AI tools, including ChatGPT and Claude, as supporting resources throughout the creative development process.

These tools helped us refine visual concepts and translate hand-drawn sketches into a consistent pixel-art direction.

All AI-assisted outputs were reviewed, adapted, and integrated by the team. The game's concept, design decisions, mechanics, and implementation were developed and managed by the team.
